Demonstration Item: Conservative Energy Exchange in a Restoring Field

A particle of mass \(m = 0.50\text{ kg}\) moves along the \(x\)-axis under the action of an ideal conservative spring force \(F(x) = -k x\), where \(k = 50.0\text{ N/m}\). At position \(x = 0.40\text{ m}\), the particle is released from rest (\(v_0 = 0\)). No dissipative forces are present.

Physical Target Question: What is the kinetic energy \(K\) and speed \(v\) of the particle when it passes through the origin \(x = 0\)?

\text{Initial state } (x_0 = 0.40\text{ m}, v_0 = 0) \longrightarrow \text{Final state } (x_1 = 0.00\text{ m}, v_1 = ?)

Step 1: Select Your Physical Assertion

Hint: Recall that the system is isolated and conservative. Compute the total mechanical energy from the initial release point: \(E = U(x_0) + K(x_0) = \frac{1}{2} k x_0^2 + 0\). At \(x = 0\), all potential energy is converted into kinetic energy: \(K = E - U(0) = \frac{1}{2} m v^2\).
Note: Your reasoning stays private in your browser's local memory and is never transmitted to any external server.

Rigorous Worked Derivation

1. Conservation of Mechanical Energy:

E_{\text{initial}} = E_{\text{final}} \implies U(x_0) + K(x_0) = U(x_1) + K(x_1)

2. Evaluating at Initial State (\(x_0 = 0.40\text{ m}\), \(v_0 = 0\)):

U_0 = \frac{1}{2} k x_0^2 = \frac{1}{2} (50.0) (0.40)^2 = 25 \times 0.16 = 4.0\text{ J}
K_0 = 0\text{ J} \implies E_{\text{tot}} = 4.0\text{ J}

3. Evaluating at Origin (\(x_1 = 0\)):

U(0) = \frac{1}{2} (50.0) (0)^2 = 0\text{ J} \implies K_1 = E_{\text{tot}} - U(0) = 4.0\text{ J}

4. Solving for Instantaneous Velocity \(v_1\):

K_1 = \frac{1}{2} m v_1^2 \implies 4.0 = \frac{1}{2} (0.50) v_1^2 = 0.25 v_1^2 \implies v_1^2 = \frac{4.0}{0.25} = 16.0 \implies v_1 = 4.0\text{ m/s}

Cognitive Feedback: Correct physical reasoning connects the zero-force point at the origin to maximum kinetic energy, rather than zero speed.
