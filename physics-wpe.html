1. Fundamental Principles: Conservative Systems & Energy

In classical Newtonian mechanics, a force \(\mathbf{F}\) is defined as conservative if the work done by the force in moving a particle between two points depends solely on the initial and final positions, and is completely independent of the trajectory taken:

\oint \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = 0 \iff \mathbf{F} = -\nabla U

In a one-dimensional system, this relates the force directly to the spatial derivative of the potential energy function \(U(x)\):

F(x) = -\frac{dU}{dx}

The negative gradient indicates that the force always acts in the direction tending to decrease the potential energy, directing the system toward stable equilibrium points where \(\frac{dU}{dx} = 0\) and \(\frac{d^2U}{dx^2} > 0\).

2. Interactive Laboratory: Potential Well & Turning Points

Observe how mechanical energy is partitioned between potential energy \(U(x)\) and kinetic energy \(K(x)\) for a particle of mass \(m = 1.0\text{ kg}\) moving in a harmonic potential well \(U(x) = \frac{1}{2} k x^2\) with \(k = 100.0\text{ N/m}\) and total energy \(E = 12.50\text{ J}\).

Potential Energy \(U(x)\) 0.00 J
Kinetic Energy \(K(x)\) 12.50 J
Instantaneous Speed \(v(x)\) 5.00 m/s

Notice: As \(x \to \pm 0.50\text{ m}\), \(U(x) \to 12.50\text{ J}\), meaning \(K(x) \to 0\text{ J}\) and \(v \to 0\text{ m/s}\). These boundary points are the turning points of the motion.

3. The Work-Energy Theorem Invariance

The Work-Energy Theorem asserts that the net work done by all forces (conservative, non-conservative, and external) acting on a point particle equals the net change in its kinetic energy:

W_{\text{net}} = \int_{x_i}^{x_f} F_{\text{net}} \, dx = \Delta K = K_f - K_i

When non-conservative forces (such as friction or drag) are absent, \(W_{\text{net}} = W_c = -\Delta U\). Rearranging yields the fundamental law of Conservation of Mechanical Energy:

\Delta K + \Delta U = 0 \implies E = K + U = \text{constant}
